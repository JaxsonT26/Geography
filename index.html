50 geography facts

The Pacific Ocean is the largest ocean on Earth.
Mount Everest is the highest mountain above sea level.
Russia spans 11 time zones.
The Amazon rainforest is the largest tropical rainforest.
The Sahara is the largest hot desert.
Antarctica is the coldest continent.
Greenland is the world’s largest island.
Lake Baikal is the deepest freshwater lake.
The Andes are the longest continental mountain range.
The Panama Canal connects the Atlantic and Pacific oceans.
The Arctic Ocean is the smallest ocean.
The Indian Ocean is the third-largest ocean.
The Equator is 0 degrees latitude.
The Prime Meridian is 0 degrees longitude.
Deserts can be hot or cold.
Latitude measures north-south position.
Longitude measures east-west position.
Japan is an island nation with many volcanoes.
The Himalayas contain many of the world’s highest mountains.
The Gobi Desert is in Asia.
The Atacama is one of the driest places on Earth.
Coral reefs support many marine species.
Tropical rainforests are warm and wet.
A watershed drains water to a common outlet.
A globe is a three-dimensional model of Earth.
Maps show Earth or part of Earth on a flat surface.
The taiga is also called the boreal forest.
The Amazon River has the greatest water flow of any river.
The Grand Canyon was carved largely by the Colorado River.
The Panama Canal is in Central America.
Monsoons are seasonal changes in wind and rainfall.
Physical geography studies natural features and processes.
Human geography studies people and places.
Antarctica is in the Southern Hemisphere.
Ocean currents move heat around the planet.
The Moon strongly affects ocean tides.
The Rocky Mountains are in North America.
The Alps stretch across several European countries.
The Nile flows through northeastern Africa.
The Great Barrier Reef lies off Australia’s northeast coast.
Volcanoes can form where tectonic plates interact.
Earth has seven commonly taught continents.
Oceania includes Australia, New Zealand and many Pacific islands.
Antarctica has no permanent native human population.
Climate describes long-term weather patterns.
Weather is the short-term state of the atmosphere.
A peninsula is land surrounded by water on three sides.
An island is land completely surrounded by water.
A strait is a narrow waterway connecting larger bodies of water.
A delta can form where a river deposits sediment near its mouth.
